# video-1 — Treatment: "One more video"

## Three candidate structures

1. **Single walk, one take, smash cut.** One continuous night walk to the crosswalk; the camera rises as he steps
   off the curb, the headlights arrive, cut to black on contact, the phone lies on the asphalt. Simple, readable muted.
2. **Countdown.** A pedestrian signal counts down 5→0 while he scrolls; on 0 the car. Clear clock, but turns the film
   into a gimmick and the timer belongs to the product's own language better saved for another video.
3. **Two worlds.** Split between the phone's bright feed and the dark street; the street wins. Too abstract to read in
   the first second.

**Chosen: 1.** The viewer sees *themselves* first (face lit by a phone, thumb scrolling), and the film turns once,
hard. The aftermath (the phone still glowing, still buzzing, on the road) says the rest without a lecture.

## Logline and arc

A student walks home lost in his phone, steps into the road without looking up, and the only thing still lit
afterwards is the screen.
Setup (walking, scrolling) → turn (headlights, horn, impact) → ending (the phone buzzes on the asphalt; "Stop
scrolling. You need to lock in."; WeLockIn).

## Benchmark

- Learn from: road-safety PSAs that cut on impact (the sound tells it, the picture refuses to show it); tilt-shift
  night-street dioramas (one warm street lamp, wet asphalt, a crossing seen from above).
- Don't take: any specific ad's shots, characters, music or slogans; no game characters, UI ornaments or names.

## Look (2.5D Pixel Art rules applied)

- Set: one city crossing at night, a box-and-cylinder diorama with pixel textures: asphalt with zebra stripes,
  two pavements, a few low buildings with lit windows, one warm sodium street lamp, a pedestrian signal.
- Two temperatures: cold blue fog and the **phone's cold glow** on the hero's face (distraction) vs. warm amber
  street lamp and windows. The car brings two **white-hot headlights**: shadows jump across the road.
- The hero wears the one saturated colour: a **red hoodie (`#C41919`)**, so the tiny sprite is findable from above.
  The brand mascot is not the victim; it appears only on the end card (real logo).
- Pedestrian signal glows amber-orange, not red (red belongs to the hero).
- Post: DOF + tilt-shift band on the hero, bloom on lamp/phone/headlights, vignette.

## Beats (playbook, ≤ 8 lines)

```
0.0–3.2   He walks, face lit blue by the phone. Dialogue box types: "Okay… one more video."
```

## Cue map / sound design

No music bed under the walk (night city ambience instead): a sparse low synth pulse at 90 BPM enters at 3.2 s and
stops dead at the impact. After the crash: tinnitus tone + silence, one phone buzz at 9.2 s and 11.0 s. A soft
two-note chime on each text card; a warm resolved chord on the end card.

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 walk | distant city, light wind | footsteps on concrete (sync to the 4-frame walk), notification pings ×2, dialogue chime | — |
| 2 crossing | + far traffic | footsteps change to asphalt, signal "tick" | low pulse in |
| 3 headlights | engine rising | horn, tyre screech | pulse rises, then cuts |
| 4 impact | — | thump + glass crack | — (hard stop) |
| 5 phone | tinnitus, then faint city | phone buzz ×2 | silence, then a low pad |
| 6 end card | — | soft chime | warm chord |

## Type

- Dialogue box (inner voice): dark plate, thin gold border, EB Garamond italic, typed, caret, chime.
- Text cards "Stop scrolling." / "You need to lock in.": Cinzel, wide tracking, cream on dark, ≥ 80 px, inside the
  TikTok safe zone.
- End card: real logo image, "Download now" (Figtree bold), "App Store · macOS · Windows" (Figtree).
- No voice-over, so no subtitles; all on-screen text is designed in.
